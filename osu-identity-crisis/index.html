Oregon State has an identity crisis, here's how to fix it

By Parker·November 23, 2026·9 min readFree · no paywall
I am not a journalist, I am just a passionate fan that knows how special Corvallis and Oregon State can be and I want the best for my team.

It is late November. JaMarcus Shephard has led us to our sixth win, avoiding a three-game skid, and a rematch against Fresno State could avenge what most fans would consider the team that derailed our comeback season. In August, a group of reporters who had never stepped foot in Corvallis had Oregon State finishing last in the new Pac-12. A four-win season would have been viewed as promising, and a bowl game was a dream. Yet as time expired on Saturday and our win over Utah State finalized what most fans in July would have considered a dream, something felt incomplete.

I have been to five games this year, four at home plus the trip to Boise State (and hopefully our bowl game, if we get the Holiday Bowl in San Diego). The sellout against WSU on October 17th was a delight to see, and still, something felt off. I think I know why.

Not long ago, Oregon State was part of a thriving power conference, with back-to-back great seasons and a coach who had the program trending in the right direction. Then conference realignment gutted the Pac-12, and that coach walked out the door (and donated all his team gear to Goodwill on the way). Now we find ourselves in a new, weaker PAC-12, coming off back-to-back bad seasons, still acting like we are in the same place we were a few years ago. We are stuck in an awkward middle, and we don't know what to do with ourselves.

The unfortunate truth is we let everyone else around us get a two-year head start (at least). At no fault to the current coaching staff or fans, we got left behind, unsure how to adjust, with no leadership to guide the way. When the adults finally got into the room (Griffen, Sheph), one short offseason was never going to be enough to reignite an entire fan base. That being said, having a nation-leading QB, a true freshman receiver who deserves to be in New York on December 12th, and a head coach who restores faith and has more energy than a hydrogen bomb can bring hope back to OSU fans, even those whose hearts were shattered by asinine realignment and a money-gouging transfer portal.

But the product on the field is a work in progress (a process many fans are fine trusting after year one), and the product around it needs a massive overhaul. Most fans are quick to complain, but few have given a full list of suggestions. So here is mine. The way out of an identity crisis is to figure out who we actually are, and I think we already know. We are the ones who stayed. We are the fan base that came back through the losing seasons, the underdog nobody expected to still be standing. Gameday should say that out loud. Here is how.

1.Fix the First Down

The game is changing. Oregon State is not a slow and methodical offense. Shephard and Dahlen want to move the ball down the field, and they want to keep defenses on their heels. I love the band's involvement and the tomahawk on first downs, but two verses is unfortunately too long. Anyone who has been to a game at Reser this year knows what I'm talking about: the band gets cut off, the fans don't stop the "BUM BUM BUM," and the "HUH" (no idea how to spell the noise that gets made) happens halfway through the next play.

I want us to keep our own uniqueness and culture, but we may need to adapt. Have the announcer call out "That's another Oregon State..." with the crowd following up "FIRST DOWN, HUH!" with the arm motion. It shortens the noise, keeps the celebration, and lets the players focus on keeping the sticks moving.

2.Fill and Protect The Dam

The student section needs to be full. Every. Single. Game. From 15 minutes in the first quarter to 0:00 in the fourth. Against Boise State or against Montana, we need our students there.

I was lucky enough to be a freshman during our last 10-win season. I remember how loud Reser was when USC came to town, and I remember the student section bumping when we came back against Oregon. Shephard has done an amazing job getting the students hyped, but we need to go a full step further. The unfortunate truth is we aren't playing USC, or Utah, or Oregon anymore. The quality of opponents and the regional rivalries that used to fuel attendance are not going to get us over the finish line.

So here's how we fix it. The student section, BeaverDam, becomes "The Dam," and The Dam must be protected. Students need a reason to come, outside of the on-field product. Guest tickets should be no more than $10, and resale is not allowed. The absolute priority is bodies in the stands. If students don't get a ticket because it sells out, we need to have a line at the onsite ticket office giving out whatever tickets haven't been sold. Companies like "ReSeat" are starting this initiative and I think it's something the university needs to partner with if we are going to see Reser full in the future. Even if we can only get Standing Room Only sections, that is better than empty seats.

When I was a student I used to show up hours before the game, before we were even allowed in the stadium, just to be at the front. We need to bring that back. Similar to how GameDay has its "first ones" merchandise, we need "First Beaver" merchandise. Additionally, the student store needs to give us Las Vegas Raiders-style gear available for purchase: fake chainsaws, helmets, shoulder pads with spikes. End zone to end zone, The Dam should be feared by any opposing offense.

3.Rebuild the Gameday Show

That leads me to my last and largest change. Similar to our first down, we need a complete overhaul of what happens on the field during run-outs, timeouts, and between quarters. If you drive 49 miles from Reser to Autzen, the production quality is night and day. UO understands that the run-out, the timeouts, the breaks between quarters, the songs, all of it, is a production meant to keep fans engaged and inspire the team on the field. I promise the Toyota sing-along, the air guitar cam, and Benny guarding a student trying to catch a TD pass for groceries (when Benny doesn't play defense) are seen as nothing but a phone break by 90% of attendees.

So what do I suggest?

The run-out

The opening chainsaw with a Beaver alumnus should be in the middle of the field, and while I like the giant fake chainsaw, let's get them a real one. They can crank the fake one to get the smoke, but they should absolutely be wielding a real chainsaw, with a real rev, that can get players and fans hyped.

Puddles gets a Harley, so how do we outdo a Harley? An Oregon State monster truck. Do I think this is realistic? No. Is it impossible? Also no. We have a race car, and I have seen crazier. Could the tires on the turf be a logistical problem? Yes, but I'm a dreamer who can adjust to reality. In a perfect world, we have an OSU monster truck with Benny in the passenger seat and our chosen alumnus in the back. As the team runs out, our alumnus climbs out and revs the chainsaw.

I love the fire shooters and the fireworks. The only thing I would suggest is more fire shooters.

A song that is actually ours

Oregon State also needs a song, one that belongs to us and that fans won't think is dumb and corny. We tried "Party in the U.S.A." and it was horrible. It had nothing to do with who we are, and the crowd could tell.

My pick is "I'm Still Standing." It is a song about getting left behind and refusing to go down, which describes the last few years of Oregon State better than anything else I can think of. When the Pac-12 collapsed, we didn't leave. The fans who stayed through the losing seasons and the realignment losses are the program. It is a song every generation in the stands already knows, it has a hook the whole stadium can sing, and unlike "Party in the U.S.A.," it has a reason to be here.

Between the first and second quarters

This is where the song lives. Between the first and second quarters, the video board plays a highlight reel set to "I'm Still Standing." It starts with the demolition of the old west side of Reser in 2022 and then runs through the greatest moments in Oregon State sports history. The stadium literally came down and we rebuilt it. Fans watch the stadium they're sitting in get rebuilt, hear a song about surviving, and see the moments that made them Beavers. That is how a quarter break becomes a tradition.

The third quarter is "The Dam Quarter"

We have a problem with the stadium starting to empty at the end of the first half, and I want to fix it. The third quarter becomes "The Dam Quarter," built entirely around the student section. Students who stay get rewarded: free shirts, a student BINGO card or raffle for something from the student store, free food, and hot chocolate on a cold November night. Whether we need a big third-quarter comeback or we're trying to keep the momentum rolling, the people who stay are the ones who change the game.

To make it work, make staying the entry requirement. Have students scan their ID or ticket at the start of the third quarter, and that scan is their raffle or BINGO entry. Otherwise they grab the prize and leave at the half.

The Dam Quarter is also where the relay race goes. The Mariners do a fish race, and I love it, but here's my idea (and it might be asking a lot). Student tickets are released on Monday, and on Tuesday all students can enter a team of three into a raffle. Four teams are picked (12 students) and compete in a relay race on the field: a 40-yard dash with Beaver feet, a football toss, and so on. The winning team gets an on-field pass for the rest of the game and a prize of some kind.

Going into the fourth quarter

Going into the fourth quarter, the video board plays the current team's hype video, narrated by Coach Shephard and the players, with the first minute and a half or so of "Blind" by Korn behind it. The stadium hears the voices of the people on the field, the music builds, and the crowd goes into the last fifteen minutes loud. Games are won late, and this is how we remind the team and the fans of that.

The field goal kick

The field goal contest we have now is slightly pathetic, so let's make it more challenging. The Blazers have always had a half-court shot for a Toyota, so let's do a 50-yard field goal for the same thing. A random fan hitting a 50-yard field goal is probably just as likely as hitting a half-court shot, but it's way more exciting than any competition we have now.

4.Get Every Seat Filled

One more piece of the puzzle is getting bodies into the stands. A new company called ReSeat Tickets takes donated tickets and assigns them to fans who sign up, with the goal of filling the stadium when tickets go unsold. If Oregon State worked with ReSeat, any seat that doesn't sell could go to a fan who wants to be there, so the crowd looks and sounds bigger on TV and in the stadium. A packed Reser is the best recruiting tool we have.

Back to Where We Started

It's late November, and we just won our sixth game, and something still feels incomplete. I don't think the answer is a new conference or a new rival. We aren't going back to the Pac-12 we used to know, and we don't need to. We need to decide who we are now and build a gameday that says it. We are the ones who stayed. We are still standing. Let's make Reser sound like it.

-Parker
